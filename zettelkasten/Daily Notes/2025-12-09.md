## 🔧 **Technical Insights**

### **Sparse Representation vs Grid Materialization**
- **Key Lesson**: Never materialize dense grids for sparse, large coordinate spaces
- **Input scale**: 496 points spanning 96,772 × 96,544 = 9.36 billion cells
- **Circular interior**: ~7.4 billion tiles (π × 48,386²)
- **Sparse boundary**: Only 588,656 tiles in HashSet (0.006% of total space)

### **AABB Sampling Optimization**
- **Problem**: Largest rectangles have billions of tiles to validate
- **Solution**: Strategic point sampling instead of exhaustive iteration
- **Sample rate**: Adaptive `max(10, dimension/100)` based on rectangle size
- **Performance**: Reduced 2.3 trillion checks to ~633 million (3,650x speedup)

### **Ray Casting Point-in-Polygon**
- **Algorithm**: Count edge crossings from point to infinity
- **Odd count** → inside polygon, **Even count** → outside
- **Implementation**: Horizontal ray to right, count intersections with edges
- **Edge cases**: Handled vertices on ray with proper y-coordinate comparisons

### **Bresenham's Line Algorithm**
- **Purpose**: Generate all integer points along polygon edges
- **Result**: 588,656 boundary tiles (vs 304K theoretical circumference)
- **Why more?**: Discrete stepping creates extra tiles at corners/diagonals

### **Geometric Strategy Differences**
- **Part 1 (unconstrained)**: Diagonal square maximizes area across circle diameter
- **Part 2 (polygon-bounded)**: Wide horizontal band through center avoids circular edges
- **Area reduction**: Part 2 only 32.4% of Part 1 due to boundary constraint

### **Mission 6 Applicability Limits**
- **Perfect for**: Small examples (8 points), bounded grids (< 1000x1000)
- **Not viable for**: Massive sparse coordinate spaces (> 10K coordinates)
- **Takeaway**: Use Mission concepts (flood fill theory) even when Grid struct doesn't fit
- **Alternative**: Geometric algorithms (ray casting) replace grid materialization
