## Day 18: Boiling Boulders

**Problem**: Calculate surface area of a 3D lava droplet made of 1x1x1 cubes (2,043 cubes).

### Results
- **Part 1**: 3,522 (total surface area) - 185µs
- **Part 2**: 2,074 (exterior surface area only) - 1.10ms
- **Combined**: 1.38ms

### Algorithm

1. **Part 1 — Neighbor counting**: For each cube, check 6 face-adjacent neighbors in a HashSet. No neighbor = exposed face. Simple O(n) with 6 lookups per cube.

2. **Part 2 — BFS flood fill from exterior**: Compute bounding box with 1 unit of padding. BFS from corner (guaranteed outside). When flood hits a lava cube face, count it. Internal air pockets are never reached by the flood.

### Key Insight: Flood Fill vs Void Detection
Instead of finding trapped air pockets (connected components of enclosed empty space), flood from outside and count what the water touches. The approach is simpler — just BFS and count — and naturally handles any void geometry.

### Interior Faces
Part 1 − Part 2 = 3,522 − 2,074 = **1,448 faces** belong to trapped air pockets inside the droplet.

## Patterns Worth Remembering

- **Exterior flood fill**: BFS from outside padded bounding box counts only exterior faces. Simpler than identifying interior voids.
- **Bounding box padding**: +1 unit on all sides ensures flood can wrap around all exterior surfaces.
- **`HashSet::insert()` double-duty**: Returns `true` if newly inserted — combines "check" and "mark" in one call.
- **3D neighbor constant**: `NEIGHBORS` array of 6 direction tuples reusable across any 3D grid problem.
