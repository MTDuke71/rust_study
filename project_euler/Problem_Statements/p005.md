# Problem 5: Smallest Multiple

**Difficulty**: Easy  
**Published**: November 30, 2001

## Problem Statement

2520 is the smallest number that can be divided by each of the numbers from 1 to 10 without any remainder.

**What is the smallest positive number that is evenly divisible by all of the numbers from 1 to 20?**

## Examples

**Numbers 1-10**:
- LCM(1, 2, 3, ..., 10) = 2520
- 2520 ÷ 1 = 2520 ✓
- 2520 ÷ 2 = 1260 ✓
- 2520 ÷ 3 = 840 ✓
- ...
- 2520 ÷ 10 = 252 ✓

## Constraints

- Result must be divisible by all numbers 1-20
- Must be the smallest such number (LCM, not just any multiple)

## Approach Hints

1. **Brute force**: Check multiples of 20 until finding one divisible by all
2. **LCM approach**: Use LCM(a, b) = (a × b) / GCD(a, b)
3. **Prime factorization**: Find highest power of each prime ≤ 20

## Mathematical Insights

### Least Common Multiple (LCM)

**Definition**: LCM of numbers is the smallest number divisible by all of them.

**Properties**:
- LCM(a, b) × GCD(a, b) = a × b
- LCM(a, b) = (a × b) / GCD(a, b)
- LCM is associative: LCM(a, b, c) = LCM(LCM(a, b), c)

### Prime Factorization Method

For numbers 1-20, find highest power of each prime:
- 2: 2^4 = 16 (from 16)
- 3: 3^2 = 9 (from 9)
- 5: 5^1 = 5 (from 5)
- 7: 7^1 = 7 (from 7)
- 11: 11^1 = 11 (from 11)
- 13: 13^1 = 13 (from 13)
- 17: 17^1 = 17 (from 17)
- 19: 19^1 = 19 (from 19)

LCM(1..20) = 2^4 × 3^2 × 5 × 7 × 11 × 13 × 17 × 19

### Why This Works

Every number 1-20 divides this product:
- 20 = 2^2 × 5 → covered by 2^4 × 5
- 18 = 2 × 3^2 → covered by 2^4 × 3^2
- 16 = 2^4 → covered by 2^4
- etc.

## Complexity

- **Brute force**: O(n × result) - slow for large ranges
- **Iterative LCM**: O(n log(max)) using Euclidean algorithm
- **Prime factorization**: O(n log n) with sieve, O(1) lookup
